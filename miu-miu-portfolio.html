Project Overview

A New Methodology
for Luxury Retail

This case study examines the integration of artificial intelligence into the traditional fashion merchandising process, using Miu Miu as a lens through which to interrogate the possibilities and limits of machine-assisted creativity in luxury fashion.

The project spans four phases: brand immersion and consumer research, trend and competitive analysis, AI-driven product generation, and final assortment curation. Each phase was designed to honor Miu Miu's irreverent yet intellectual DNA while pushing against the boundaries of conventional product development.

The resulting assortment reflects a dialogue between algorithmic pattern recognition and human editorial judgment — a methodology that proposes a new role for the merchandiser as curator, critic, and creative director of AI-generated outputs.

Brand + Consumer Research

Understanding
the Miu Miu World

Brand Identity

Founded in 1993 as Prada's sister label, Miu Miu occupies a distinct position in the luxury landscape — youthful yet knowing, playful yet cerebral. The brand champions an unapologetically feminine vocabulary inflected with intellectual provocation and nostalgic irreverence.

Under Miuccia Prada's creative direction, Miu Miu has become a cultural touchstone for a generation of consumers who approach fashion as a form of personal philosophy. Each collection reads as a meditation on femininity, memory, and the politics of dressing.

Consumer Profile

The Miu Miu customer is intellectually curious, culturally engaged, and financially independent. She moves between art world openings, university campuses, and fashion weeks with equal fluency — and she dresses with the deliberate self-consciousness of someone who understands the semiotics of clothing.

Three distinct consumer archetypes were identified through research: the Cultural Insider, the Aspirational Scholar, and the Ironist — each engaging with the brand through a different emotional and aesthetic register.

Cultural Insider

Age 24–34. Deeply embedded in contemporary art, film, and music. Treats Miu Miu as a statement of belonging within a specific cultural milieu.

Aspirational Scholar

Age 18–26. University-educated, internationally minded. Drawn to Miu Miu's intellectual posturing and the social capital of the brand's youthful cachet.

The Ironist

Age 28–42. Appreciates Miu Miu's capacity for self-aware humor and subversion. Buys selectively, with a curator's eye for the most conceptually charged pieces.

Reading the Season

Macro Trend Landscape

The research phase identified four overarching macro trends shaping the Spring/Summer market: Post-Digital Nostalgia, Quiet Sensuality, Neo-Academicism, and Functional Luxury. Each was evaluated for alignment with Miu Miu's aesthetic DNA and commercial viability within the brand's existing customer base.

AI Assortment Development

Products Born from
Algorithmic Intuition

Using a combination of generative image models and large language models trained on Miu Miu's visual and conceptual archive, a preliminary assortment of 72 AI-generated product concepts was developed. These were subsequently edited through a rigorous editorial process — informed by commercial metrics, trend relevance, and brand coherence — to arrive at the final 48-SKU assortment.

The AI generation process was treated not as a replacement for human creativity but as a form of accelerated moodboarding — a way of rapidly externalizing possibilities that would otherwise require weeks of sampling and prototyping.

Process + Implementation

From Prompt to
Product

The methodology developed for this project establishes a repeatable framework for AI-assisted assortment planning in luxury fashion — one that acknowledges both the generative power of machine learning models and the irreplaceable role of human editorial judgment.

Each step was documented in detail to allow for iteration and refinement, with particular attention to the prompt engineering strategies that proved most effective in capturing Miu Miu's distinctive aesthetic language.

01

Brand Immersion & Archival Research

A comprehensive visual and textual analysis of Miu Miu's design vocabulary, spanning 30 years of runway collections, advertising campaigns, and editorial coverage. This established the ground truth against which all AI outputs would be evaluated.

02

Consumer & Market Research

Qualitative consumer interviews, quantitative survey data, and social listening analysis combined to build a detailed picture of the Miu Miu customer's evolving needs, aesthetic preferences, and purchasing behavior.

03

AI Prompt Development & Generation

An iterative process of prompt engineering, model selection, and output curation. Over 400 AI-generated concepts were produced across multiple models before a refined prompt library was established that could reliably generate on-brand outputs.

04

Editorial Edit & Assortment Curation

The AI-generated concepts were subjected to a rigorous editorial process, evaluating each against criteria of brand coherence, commercial viability, trend alignment, and category balance to arrive at the final 48-SKU assortment.

05

Financial Planning & Buy Simulation

A simulated open-to-buy analysis was conducted for the final assortment, with suggested retail prices, margin targets, and volume projections modeled against comparable Miu Miu SKU performance data.

Reflection

What the Machine
Cannot Know

On the Limits and Possibilities of AI in Luxury Merchandising

The most significant insight to emerge from this project was not about the capabilities of artificial intelligence but about the nature of human editorial judgment. The AI tools used throughout this process were remarkably capable of generating visually coherent, trend-appropriate product concepts — but they could not, in any meaningful sense, understand why a particular piece of lace trim or an unexpected fabric combination might feel right for Miu Miu in this particular cultural moment.

"The algorithm sees pattern. The merchandiser sees meaning. The most generative space in this project existed at the intersection of both."

This distinction — between pattern recognition and meaning-making — proved to be the central creative tension of the project. The AI could identify that Miu Miu frequently uses broderie anglaise in spring collections; it could not understand that this choice is always in dialogue with notions of girlhood, nostalgia, and the politics of innocence.

The practical implications for the fashion industry are significant. AI-assisted assortment development has the potential to dramatically accelerate the early ideation phase of the product development process. But the human editorial role — far from being diminished — becomes more important, not less. The merchandiser of the future will need to be a sophisticated cultural critic as well as a commercial strategist.

If I were to repeat this project, I would invest more time in developing a richer prompt vocabulary — one that captures not just the visual language of the brand but its conceptual and cultural registers. I would also involve more consumer voices in the editorial edit, using real customer response data to test the AI-generated concepts against actual purchasing intent.

Key Learnings

What Worked

AI generation proved most effective when given highly specific stylistic constraints derived from archival research. The combination of visual reference images and detailed textual prompts consistently outperformed either approach alone.

What Didn't

Generic prompts referencing only the brand name produced generic outputs. The AI had clearly ingested mass-market interpretations of the brand rather than the brand itself, requiring significant prompt refinement to overcome.

Future Directions

Fine-tuning a model on curated brand-specific training data — runway imagery, campaign photography, and critical writing — would likely yield significantly more on-brand outputs from the outset.
